## Projection

This is a projection, not a stored forecast. Intake is held at 329.767 GB/day. The 90-day yield is applied to the open batch and to the pool. A new batch is posted only after the previous one deletes (one open batch per kind). Day 0 is 2026-11-13T18:43Z. Each step applies `rate × days` of intake, then the deletion, then the next ask from the new gap. TB = 1e12 bytes. Figures below are rounded to 3 decimals; the shortfall is the exact difference.

Required deletion over 60 days = gap − rate × 60 = 63.180 TB. Expected deletion = yield × (7,677,954,075,421 + 1,881,676,268,710) = 8.417 TB. **Shortfall = 54.762 TB.** Day-60 free space 77.727 TB, used 85.333%, gap 54.762 TB. HaynesTower does not return to 75% within 60 days.

The pool is fully committed on the batch posted at day 21 (2026-12-04) and those bytes delete at day 28 (2026-12-11). After that the measured stock is gone. Intake over 60 days is 19.786 TB, and with nothing left to delete, use gets worse. Raising the ceiling does not change the day-60 total. The pool is the limit (Q-06: this assumes the rules do not refill it). Historical skips are not in the yield, so 54.762 TB is not a high estimate of the gap (Q-07). If the open batch, which had 0 saves at the reading, were deleted in full instead of at the 90-day yield, the shortfall would be about 0.22 TB smaller. The figure here uses the yield.

## Open questions

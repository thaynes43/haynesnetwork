## Projection (estimated, conditional)

Run `node --experimental-strip-types .agents/context/2026-11-14-852-projection.mjs`.
It imports the installed `forecastFreeSpace` source, makes no request and checks the input counts.
Computed on 2026-11-14 from the measured series plus replica deletions: unadjusted rate
−212.243750 GB/day; adjusted rate −329.766947 GB/day, hence intake 329.766947 GB/day.
The rate is a statistic of measured history; carrying it forward is an estimate.

Assumptions: constant measured intake and yield; no pool refill or new protections; current 75%
target, seven-day window, largest-first 50-movie cap and zero TV pool; 30-day rolling time constant,
measured floor and hard ceiling. The already posted batch deletes once at day 7. Each later batch
is proposed after that sweep and deletes seven days later. This rounds the current remaining
window to seven days and ignores hourly scheduling slivers; it is not an operational schedule.
Titles saved are removed from future supply (the pool drains by proposed bytes, free space rises
by yield times proposed bytes). Nothing counts the open batch twice.

Unlimited supply cannot be measured. Q-17 stays open. Two explicit estimates bracket its meaning:
(1) a mathematical favourable upper bound with enough fitting titles to fill the hard ceiling
under the title cap every window; (2) fresh copies of today's largest fitting 50-title selection,
1,358,996,419,179 bytes per window. Neither asserts that such a pool will exist.
Today's finite supply is the measured sorted list of unposted eligible sizes, retained without
names or viewing in the input JSON. That pool is fully posted on projected day 56 and its final
batch deletes on projected day 63. The precise timestamps are not predicted.
